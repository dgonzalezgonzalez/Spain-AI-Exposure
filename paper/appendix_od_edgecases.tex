\subsection*{Automated Handling of Occupational Matching Edge Cases}
\label{sec:v1_edgecase_matching}

Semantic similarity can assign a Spanish occupation to a U.S. occupation that shares its vocabulary while performing different work. We assess whether such assignments account for the baseline estimates by verifying potentially problematic matches before assigning exposure. This exercise retains Anthropic's published occupation-level exposure values and changes the crosswalk used to assign them to Spanish CNO4 occupations. We refer to the resulting measure as \emph{verified-match observed exposure}. The procedure has four steps.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textit{Initial semantic matching.} We begin with the baseline nearest-neighbor crosswalk described in Section~\ref{sec:mapping}. The same multilingual embeddings rank the 756 U.S. occupations in the exposure catalogue for each of the 502 Spanish occupations.

\item \textit{Screening potential mismatches.} Local TEV evaluates the Spanish occupation's title, definition, tasks, included examples, and exclusions together with the baseline U.S. occupation's title and description. It returns a probability that the proposed match materially misrepresents core duties or responsibility and skill level. We flag cases with a probability of at least 0.5; a flag initiates correction and does not establish that the baseline match is incorrect.

\item \textit{Required-choice matching.} In the same request, TEV compares the 24 highest cosine-ranked candidates and selects a substantive equivalent or the closest available proxy. For a flagged occupation, we reuse this selection as its automatic correction; unflagged occupations retain the baseline match. The screening and matching questions are independent and cannot see one another's answers, although both see the shared occupation evidence and candidate schema. Neither exposure values nor Spanish labor-market outcomes enter these requests. The selected U.S. occupation's published exposure is joined afterward. This shortlist procedure differs from the full-catalogue probability measure and the cloud verification used in the Jev release.

\item \textit{Crosswalk review.} An assistant-led source review inspects the complete automatic crosswalk anew and documents clear residual conflicts in duties or occupational role, including unflagged cases. The previous release's manual overrides are not carried over automatically. Borderline cross-national proxies are retained and their limitations documented. \input{paper/tev_edgecase_counts.tex}
\end{enumerate}
