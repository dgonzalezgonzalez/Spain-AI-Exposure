Finally, we examine whether the baseline findings depend on potentially misleading occupational matches. Online Appendix~\ref{sec:v1_edgecase_matching} uses TEV to screen the baseline crosswalk, reuses its fixed-shortlist matching output for flagged cases, and applies a documented assistant-led source review to all 502 occupations. The resulting crosswalk changes 123 baseline codes, including 56 explicit review corrections. Online Appendix Table~\ref{tab:v1_edgecase_robustness} implies unemployment increases of approximately 1.6 percent during the adjustment period and 2.4 percent later per 10 percentage points of reviewed-match observed exposure; both coefficients are statistically significant at the 5 percent level. Contract estimates remain close to zero and imprecise. Full-sample pre-treatment tests reject for both outcomes, so this exercise does not resolve the identifying concerns. Retained cross-national proxies and the absence of independent human adjudication also limit its interpretation as a matching-quality check.
